## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Standard Operating Procedure defines the correct methods for lubricant selection, application, replenishment, and change-out for all rotating mechanical equipment in the plant. Following this procedure correctly is the single most effective means of preventing bearing and gear failure. Poor or incorrect lubrication accounts for approximately 40% of premature bearing failures in centrifugal pumps and industrial gearboxes at this site.

This SOP applies to:
- Centrifugal pump bearing housings (oil-lubricated type)
- Industrial gearbox oil sumps
- Grease-packed bearings in fans, conveyors, and auxiliary drives
- Coupling lubrication (where grease-packed flexible disc couplings are used)

It does not cover engine lubrication, hydraulic system fluids, or compressor cylinder oil — these are covered in separate SOPs.

## 2. Safety Requirements

> **DANGER:** Before performing any lubrication task that requires opening a bearing housing, removing an oil filler plug, or applying grease to a fitting in proximity to a rotating shaft, isolate and lock out the drive motor per SOP-LOTO-01. Failure to do so exposes personnel to entanglement with rotating shafts, which causes fatal or life-altering injuries.

> **WARNING:** Many lubricating oils operate at temperatures above 70 °C during normal service. Allow the equipment to cool to below 40 °C before opening drain plugs or fill ports to prevent scalding burns. Wear heat-resistant gloves and safety glasses at all times when handling hot oil.

> **CAUTION:** Lubricants are slippery. Any spill must be cleaned up immediately using the spill kit in the maintenance bay. Oil on a hard floor creates a slip hazard that can cause a serious fall.

> **NOTICE:** Mixing oils of different base stocks or different additive packages (even if they appear similar) can cause chemical incompatibility, resulting in sludge formation, foam, and accelerated wear. When in doubt, drain and flush before refilling with the new oil.

**Required PPE for all lubrication tasks:**

| PPE Item | Minimum Requirement |
|---|---|
| Safety glasses | EN 166 rated, with side shields |
| Chemical-resistant gloves | Nitrile, minimum 0.2 mm thickness |
| Safety footwear | ISO 20345 S1P rated |
| Overalls | Fire-resistant for hydrocarbon service areas |

## 3. Approved Lubricants

### 3.1 Approved Oil List

The following oils are approved for use on the equipment covered by this SOP. Substitution of any oil outside this list requires a written Technical Deviation Request (TDR) approved by Reliability Engineering.

| Equipment Type | Oil Grade | Approved Products | Change Interval |
|---|---|---|---|
| Centrifugal pump bearing housing | ISO VG 46 mineral | Shell Tellus S2 M 46, Mobil DTE 25, Total Azolla ZS 46 | 3 months / 2 000 h |
| Industrial gearbox (standard) | ISO VG 150 mineral (CLP) | Shell Omala S2 G 150, Mobil Gear 629, Total Carter SH 150 | 3 months / 2 000 h |
| Industrial gearbox (high temp >70 °C) | ISO VG 220 synthetic PAO | Shell Omala S4 GX 220, Mobil SHC Gear 220 | 6 months / 4 000 h |
| Grease-packed bearings (general) | NLGI 2 mineral | Shell Gadus S2 V220 2, Mobilux EP 2, Total Multis EP 2 | 6 months |
| Grease-packed bearings (high temp) | NLGI 2 polyurea | Mobil Polyrex EM, Shell Stamina RL 2 | 6 months |

### 3.2 Lubricant Identification and Storage

All lubricants must be stored in clearly labelled, sealed containers in the dedicated lubricant store. Containers must show: lubricant name, ISO grade, approved equipment list, date received, and date opened. No unlabelled containers are permitted in the lubricant store.

Lubricants shall not be stored directly on the floor. Use pallet racking or shelving. Maintain separation between different lubricant types to prevent cross-contamination during dispensing.

## 4. Oil Replenishment Procedure

### 4.1 Checking Oil Level (Centrifugal Pump Bearing Housing)

**Procedure LUB-01-A — Oil Level Check:**

1. With the pump stopped (or with an adequate guard in place if observing on a running machine from an external sight glass), observe the bearing housing oil level sight glass.
2. The acceptable range is from 5 mm below the midpoint to 5 mm above the midpoint of the sight glass.
3. If the level is below the midpoint, top up per Procedure LUB-01-B.
4. If the oil appears milky, dark, or contains visible particulate, initiate an oil change per Procedure LUB-01-C regardless of the level.
5. Record the observation (level, oil appearance) in the daily rounds sheet.

### 4.2 Topping Up Bearing Housing Oil

**Procedure LUB-01-B — Oil Top-Up:**

1. Perform lockout/tagout per SOP-LOTO-01 if the filler plug is within 300 mm of a rotating shaft.
2. Clean the area around the filler/vent plug with a rag.
3. Remove the filler plug (3/8" BSP). Do not allow any contaminants to fall into the housing.
4. Using a clean, dedicated pump and transfer hose (labelled for ISO VG 46 only), add oil in small increments, checking the sight glass between additions.
5. Stop filling when the oil level reaches the midpoint of the sight glass.
6. Replace the filler plug. Torque to 20–25 Nm.
7. Record the quantity added and the date in the PM log.

### 4.3 Full Oil Change

**Procedure LUB-01-C — Oil Change:**

1. Perform lockout/tagout per SOP-LOTO-01.
2. Allow the bearing housing to cool to below 40 °C.
3. Place a labelled drain pan under the drain plug.
4. Remove the drain plug. Allow 10 minutes for complete drainage.
5. Inspect the drained oil (refer to Table in Section 3.1 for interpretation of oil appearance). Retain a sample if contamination is suspected.
6. Refit the drain plug with fresh PTFE tape. Torque to 35–40 Nm (centrifugal pump) or 50–60 Nm (gearbox).
7. Fill with the approved oil for this equipment from a clean, closed container. Fill to the midpoint of the sight glass.
8. Record the oil change: date, equipment ID, oil product, quantity used, and any observations in the PM log.

## 5. Grease Application Procedure

### 5.1 Re-greasing Bearings

**Procedure LUB-01-D — Bearing Re-greasing:**

1. Confirm the grease nipple type matches the grease gun coupler. Using the wrong coupler can damage the nipple valve.
2. Wipe the grease nipple with a clean rag.
3. Apply grease in short pumping strokes (1–2 pumps per cycle) while monitoring for grease exiting the purge point (if fitted). Stop when clean grease begins to emerge at the purge point.
4. Wipe off any excess grease from around the nipple and seal area.
5. Record the re-greasing event, quantity used, and date.

> **CAUTION:** Over-greasing is as damaging as under-greasing. Excess grease causes churning, heat generation, and seal damage. Apply only the quantity specified in the equipment manufacturer's data sheet, not until the grease gun becomes hard to push.

## 6. Lubrication Record Keeping

All lubrication tasks must be recorded in the Computerised Maintenance Management System (CMMS) under the relevant work order. The minimum data to record are:

- Equipment asset ID
- Date and time
- Task performed (check, top-up, or full change)
- Oil/grease product name and batch/lot number
- Quantity used (litres for oil, grams for grease)
- Technician name and signature
- Any abnormal observations (colour, contamination, unusual smell)

Incomplete lubrication records are a non-conformance under the plant ISO 55001 asset management system and will be raised in the monthly maintenance KPI review.
